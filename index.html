John Locke

1632 – 1704

English Philosopher

"The Champion of Natural Rights"

Tap to Flip ⤵

Key Ideas

  • Believed all people are born with natural rights (life, liberty, and property).
  • Proposed the concept of the social contract, where government gets its authority from the "consent of the governed."
  • Argued citizens have a right to alter or overthrow a government that fails to protect natural rights.

Influence on U.S.

John Locke directly inspired Thomas Jefferson when writing the Declaration of Independence. Locke’s theory of natural rights was adapted into "life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness," forming the foundational belief that American government exists to serve and protect its citizens.

Historical Facts

  • Wrote Two Treatises of Government in 1689.
  • Was a trained physician and served as a personal doctor before writing his famous political philosophy works.

Montesquieu

1689 – 1755

French Judge & Thinker

"Architect of the Three Branches"

Tap to Flip ⤵

Key Ideas

  • Developed the concept of the separation of powers to prevent tyranny.
  • Introduced the system of checks and balances, where each branch restrains the others.
  • Believed laws should adapt to the specific social, geographic, and political needs of a country.

Influence on U.S.

Montesquieu’s framework directly shaped the structure of the U.S. Constitution. The Constitutional Convention divided the federal government into three distinct branches—legislative, executive, and judicial—to ensure no single branch or leader could gain absolute control.

Historical Facts

  • Published his landmark political masterpiece, The Spirit of the Laws, in 1748.
  • Inherited the official title "Baron de La Brède et de Montesquieu" along with a seat in the regional parliament of Bordeaux.

Voltaire

1694 – 1778

French Writer & Critic

"Defender of Free Speech"

Tap to Flip ⤵

Key Ideas

  • Fiercely advocated for freedom of speech, freedom of religion, and freedom of the press.
  • Supported the separation of church and state to protect personal freedom and reduce religious conflict.
  • Used wit, satire, and reason to criticize religious intolerance, censorship, and corruption.

Influence on U.S.

Voltaire’s defense of civil liberties heavily influenced James Madison and the Framers during the creation of the Bill of Rights. His core principles are directly reflected in the First Amendment, which protects freedom of speech, religion, press, and assembly.

Historical Facts

  • Wrote Treatise on Tolerance (1763) and the famous satirical novel Candide (1759).
  • Spent time imprisoned in the famous Bastille prison in Paris twice due to his outspoken writings.
